# V1.1 — Strategic Squad Optimization, Full GUI Integration & End-to-End ML Analysis

**Status:** Planned  
**Target branch:** `version_11`  
**Predecessor:** V1.0.0  
**Primary objective:** Extend FPL Manager from a strong gameweek/transfer decision engine into a complete strategic squad-construction and evaluation platform.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

V1.0 establishes a stable, reproducible and measurable FPL decision-support platform. The quantitative core is frozen at `v0.9.1-frozen`, production optimizers have explicit exact/heuristic characterization, independent exact reference solvers exist for bounded synthetic cases, historical evaluation is point-in-time, and decision outcomes can be audited.

V1.1 changes the scope of the system.

The central question becomes:

> **Given that squad construction itself is a strategic decision, can FPL Manager construct the right starting state, allow a human to refine it through explicit constraints, and then demonstrate through leakage-free historical replay that the resulting starting state improves downstream decisions over multiple Gameweeks?**

V1.1 therefore has exactly three major pillars:

1. **Engine — Strategic Squad Construction**
   - Generalized constrained squad optimization.
   - Strategic initial-team optimizer.
   - Strategic Wildcard optimizer.
   - Reuse/generalization of the existing Free Hit optimizer.
   - Multiple strategic solutions rather than one supposedly universal “optimal” squad.
   - Hard constraints, soft preferences, locked players and excluded players.
   - Multi-Gameweek strategic horizons.
   - Integration with the existing multi-GW planner.

2. **Full GUI Integration**
   - Make strategic squad construction a first-class user workflow.
   - Display multiple strategic candidates.
   - Allow users to lock, exclude or prefer players.
   - Re-optimize immediately under the modified constraints.
   - Support both optimizer → human and human → optimizer workflows.
   - Expose strategic trade-offs, objective values, assumptions and provenance.
   - Make the resulting workflow usable without requiring CLI interaction.

3. **Full ML Analysis**
   - Re-run and extend the V0.7–V0.9 analytical methodology around the new strategic starting-state problem.
   - Generate historical initial squads and Wildcard squads using only information available at the historical decision deadline.
   - Evaluate multiple strategic starting states over future Gameweeks.
   - Compare starting-state quality, prediction quality and decision quality.
   - Perform multi-season walk-forward analysis.
   - Quantify interactions between strategic squad construction, the V1.0 prediction engine and downstream decision-making.
   - Identify whether improvements come from the starting squad, the predictive model, the decision engine, or their interactions.

V1.1 is therefore **not simply a new optimizer release**. It is an end-to-end strategic evaluation release.

# 2. Product Thesis

The existing system is primarily optimized around the question:

> “Given my current squad, what should I do next?”

V1.1 adds the preceding question:

> “What squad should I start from?”

This distinction is fundamental.

A Free Hit is primarily a one-Gameweek optimization problem:

> Given a temporary squad and one Gameweek, maximize the selected Gameweek objective.

A Wildcard is different:

> Given the current squad and a transfer-reset opportunity, construct a squad that is strategically strong over a future horizon.

Initial squad construction is different again:

> Given the season-start budget and no existing squad, construct a squad that performs well over an initial horizon while preserving future flexibility.

These should share common optimization infrastructure but should not be treated as the same optimization problem.

# 3. V1.1 Design Principles

## 3.1 Separate squad construction from weekly decision-making

The architecture must distinguish:

```text
Strategic Squad Construction
        ↓
Initial / Wildcard / Free Hit Squad
        ↓
Weekly Decision Engine
        ↓
Lineup / Captain / Transfer / Chip decisions
        ↓
Observed outcome
```

The strategic optimizer creates a state. The decision engine operates on that state.

## 3.2 “Optimal” is strategy-dependent

There may be several valid strategic optima.

Examples:

- maximum expected value;
- high floor / low downside;
- high ceiling / upside;
- rank defence;
- rank chase;
- future flexibility;
- balanced strategy.

Therefore V1.1 should prefer a **candidate set of strategically strong squads** over a single universal `optimal_team`.

Candidate solutions may be:

- optimal for different objectives;
- near-optimal under the same objective;
- structurally different but close in strategic value.

The system must preserve this distinction.

## 3.3 Human constraints are part of the optimization problem

A user saying:

> “I want Haaland and Donnarumma.”

is not a manual edit to an already-computed squad.

It is an optimization constraint.

The optimizer should solve:

```text
maximize strategic_utility(squad, horizon)

subject to:
    squad_size = 15
    budget <= available_budget
    positional_constraints
    club_constraints
    locked_players ⊆ squad
    excluded_players ∩ squad = ∅
```

The same mechanism must work whether constraints originate from:

- the GUI;
- the CLI;
- an existing squad;
- a user profile;
- a strategic preset;
- an optimizer result.

## 3.4 Preserve V1.0’s exact/heuristic distinction

V1.1 must not claim global optimality merely because an optimizer returns a result.

Production strategic optimization may remain heuristic or bounded.

The architecture should expose:

- `algorithm`;
- `is_exact_global_optimum`;
- `optimality_guarantee`;
- objective definition;
- search limits;
- candidate count;
- constraints;
- provenance.

Small synthetic problems should continue to have independent exact-reference validation where practical.

## 3.5 No future leakage

This is a non-negotiable requirement for the ML/research pillar.

For every historical initial-squad or Wildcard experiment:

```text
historical deadline
        ↓
information available at that deadline
        ↓
feature reconstruction
        ↓
prediction
        ↓
strategic optimizer
        ↓
candidate squads
        ↓
future replay
        ↓
actual outcome
```

The optimizer must never see future points, future minutes, future transfers, future injuries, future fixture outcomes, future price changes, or future ownership information unless it was historically available.

# 4. Major Pillar I — Engine

## P0 — Generalized Strategic Squad Optimization Framework

### Objective

Create a reusable optimization layer supporting:

- initial squad construction;
- Wildcard;
- Free Hit;
- constrained re-optimization;
- future strategic horizons.

### Requirements

The engine must support:

- squad size;
- budget;
- position quotas;
- club limits;
- locked players;
- excluded players;
- soft player preferences;
- strategy/risk profile;
- horizon;
- transfer flexibility;
- bench value;
- captaincy opportunity;
- future fixture quality;
- future squad flexibility.

### Constraint types

**Hard constraints** must always be satisfied:

- budget;
- squad size;
- positional requirements;
- maximum players per club;
- locked players;
- excluded players;
- chip-specific legality.

**Soft constraints** may influence the objective:

- player preference;
- preferred club;
- preferred position;
- fixture preference;
- future flexibility;
- differential preference;
- risk preference.

The engine must clearly distinguish hard constraints from soft preferences.

# 5. Strategic Objective Model

V1.1 should introduce an explicit strategic objective rather than hiding future considerations inside arbitrary weights.

A conceptual objective may contain:

```text
StrategicValue =
    expected_points_over_horizon
  + future_transfer_flexibility
  + fixture_value
  + captaincy_option_value
  + squad_structure_value
  + bench_value
  + strategy_adjustments
  - expected_transfer_cost
  - structural_risk
```

The exact implementation must be validated experimentally.

Every new component must be:

1. measurable;
2. backtestable;
3. ablatable;
4. removable if it does not improve decision-level outcomes.

# 7. Initial Squad Optimizer

## Objective

Construct the best starting squad for a configurable initial horizon.

Default horizon should be configurable, for example:

```text
GW1–GW4
GW1–GW6
GW1–GW8
```

The optimizer should consider:

- expected points;
- expected minutes;
- fixtures;
- captaincy;
- squad structure;
- bench strength;
- price/value;
- future transfer flexibility;
- risk profile;
- expected early transfer pressure.

The objective is **not** simply `maximize GW1 xP`, nor merely `maximize sum(GW1...GW6 xP)` without considering the state produced for later Gameweeks.

The research task is to determine which strategic formulation produces the best downstream decision outcomes.

---

# 8. Wildcard Strategic Optimizer

The Wildcard optimizer starts from an existing historical/current squad and constructs a new squad.

Inputs include:

- current squad;
- bank;
- current Gameweek;
- available players;
- point-in-time predictions;
- fixtures;
- current strategic state;
- locked players;
- excluded players;
- desired horizon;
- strategy profile.

The optimizer should consider:

- future fixture runs;
- expected points over the horizon;
- future transfer flexibility;
- squad structure;
- bench;
- captaincy;
- expected future moves;
- strategic risk.

The Wildcard optimizer should not simply be the Free Hit optimizer with a larger horizon.

---

# 9. Free Hit Generalization

The existing Free Hit optimizer is a useful foundation.

V1.1 should generalize it so that the same squad-construction framework can accept:

```text
chip = free_hit
horizon = 1 GW
```

while Wildcard can use:

```text
chip = wildcard
horizon = N GWs
```

and initial selection:

```text
chip = initial
horizon = N GWs
```

The optimization framework should be shared where appropriate, while objectives and state-transition semantics remain explicit.

# 10. Constraint-Driven Re-Optimization

This is a core V1.1 capability.

Example:

Optimizer proposes:

```text
Haaland
Donnarumma
Palmer
...
```

User selects:

```text
LOCK:
Haaland
Donnarumma
```

The system then solves:

```text
best strategic squad
subject to:
    Haaland ∈ squad
    Donnarumma ∈ squad
```

The user can subsequently:

```text
LOCK
UNLOCK
EXCLUDE
PREFER
```

and trigger another optimization.

The optimizer must retain the previous result and explain what changed.

# 11. Constraint Impact Analysis

# 22. Historical Initial-Squad Backtest

For each historical season:

1. reconstruct the point-in-time state before GW1;
2. reconstruct all information available at the time;
3. generate candidate initial squads;
4. record all strategic objectives and constraints;
5. replay the season forward;
6. run the weekly decision engine;
7. record actual outcomes;
8. attribute errors.

No future information may influence squad construction.

---

# 23. Historical Wildcard Backtest

For each historical Wildcard opportunity:

1. reconstruct the squad immediately before the Wildcard;
2. reconstruct the historical information state;
3. run the strategic Wildcard optimizer;
4. generate multiple candidates;
5. record objective values;
6. replay the next N Gameweeks;
7. run the normal decision engine;
8. compare realized results.

Repeat across multiple seasons and Wildcard contexts.

---

# 30. Counterfactual Error Attribution

V1.1 should distinguish:

```text
Starting-State Error
Prediction Error
Decision Error
Interaction Error
```

For example:

> A player was never transferred in because the starting squad did not contain the relevant structure.

That is different from:

> The player was correctly available but predicted incorrectly.

And different again from:

> The prediction was correct but the decision engine selected another player.

The attribution system should identify these separately.

---

# 31. Strategic Regret

Introduce a strategic regret concept.

For a historical starting decision:

```text
Strategic Regret =
    realized outcome of best hindsight-feasible candidate
    -
    realized outcome of selected candidate
```

This must be used carefully.

The hindsight candidate is an evaluation reference, not something the production optimizer is allowed to see.

Reports must explicitly distinguish:

- decision-time feasible candidates;
- hindsight oracle candidates;
- production-selected candidates.

---

# 32. Multiple Optimal / Near-Optimal Solutions

The ML analysis should explicitly measure solution multiplicity.

For each historical optimization:

- number of optimal solutions if exact;
- number of near-optimal solutions within a defined objective tolerance;
- structural diversity;
- common players;
- strategy-specific differences.

A strategic optimizer should not be judged as failing merely because it chooses a different squad with essentially equivalent strategic value.

# 44. Performance Requirements

Strategic optimization can be substantially more expensive than one-GW optimization.

Therefore:

- use bounded search where necessary;
- cache reusable predictions;
- cache fixture/horizon computations;
- avoid recomputing unchanged constraints;
- support candidate limits;
- expose search configuration;
- ensure GUI interaction remains responsive.

The system must not attempt unrestricted brute-force search over the full real-world FPL search space merely to claim exactness.

Independent exact reference solvers remain bounded validation tools.

# 48. What V1.1 Should Prove

The release should ultimately answer three questions.

### Question 1 — Engine

> Can FPL Manager construct strategically meaningful squads for initial selection and Wildcard, rather than merely maximizing a single Gameweek?

### Question 2 — Product

> Can a human interactively guide that optimization through locks, exclusions, preferences and strategic choices without leaving the GUI?

### Question 3 — Science

> Does starting from strategically constructed squads measurably improve downstream decision quality when evaluated with the same strict point-in-time, walk-forward methodology established in V0.7–V0.9?

The third question is the most important.

V1.1 should not be considered successful merely because it produces more sophisticated-looking squads.

It succeeds if the complete, leakage-free experiment demonstrates where strategic squad construction helps, where it does not, and under which objectives, horizons and constraints the differences matter.

---

# 49. Explicit Non-Goals

V1.1 does **not** need to:

- solve every strategic optimization problem exactly;
- replace the V1.0 prediction engine wholesale;
- introduce autonomous LLM decision-making;
- support every possible external AI provider;
- implement a fully autonomous FPL manager;
- guarantee future points;
- eliminate uncertainty;
- replace human strategic preferences.

The purpose of V1.1 is to make **strategic squad construction and its empirical evaluation first-class capabilities**.

---

# 50. Relationship to V1.2

V1.1 creates the foundation for the subsequent AI/LLM research release.

V1.2 can then investigate:

- multi-provider LLM infrastructure;
- LLM model benchmarking;
- qualitative strategy analysis;
- closed-loop LLM evaluation;
- human-in-the-loop experiment orchestration;
- AI-assisted strategic reasoning;
- additional ML research.

The important dependency is:

```text
V1.0
Reliable decision engine
        ↓
V1.1
Strategic squad construction
+ GUI
+ end-to-end ML evaluation
        ↓
V1.2
AI/LLM-assisted strategic decision support
```

This ordering keeps the quantitative decision system independently useful before adding more AI complexity.

---

# 51. Final V1.1 Position

V1.0 made FPL Manager a stable decision-support platform.

V1.1 should make it a **strategic squad-construction and evaluation platform**.

The defining capability is no longer:

> “Tell me which player to transfer.”

It becomes:

> **“Help me construct the best strategic state, let me express my own constraints and preferences, show me the meaningful alternatives, and then prove through historical analysis what those starting decisions actually changed.”**

That is the intended scope of V1.1.
