## 2. Root Cause Analysis

Gameweek-by-gameweek trace analysis of the 2023-24 and 2024-25 simulation trajectories identified four distinct architectural bottlenecks in V1.2's transfer and squad execution:

### 2.1 The Candidate Pool Funnel Bottleneck in `solve_transfers`
In `DecisionEngineV12.decide_transfers`:
```python
recs, _ = solve_transfers(
    num_transfers=k,
    squad_players=squad_opt,
    candidate_pool=cand_pool,
    ...,
    max_results=5,
)
```
1. `solve_transfers` scored and ranked candidate transfers using the unweighted squad sum delta.
2. Only the top **5** recommendations from this unweighted search were returned.
3. Only *after* that pruning step did `DecisionEngineV12` re-evaluate those 5 moves using the asymmetric lineup objective.
4. **The pathology:** If a high-value Starting XI upgrade was ranked 6th–20th in flat squad sum due to bench or budget permutations, `DecisionEngineV12` **never saw it**. High-leverage starting moves were discarded before lineup evaluation occurred.

### 2.2 Goalkeeper Transfer Churn & Wasted Free Transfers
In the 2023-24 replay, V1.2 spent **5 full free transfers** swapping goalkeepers (GW09/11/17/32/38). Because goalkeepers are inexpensive (£4.5m–£5.5m) and fixture difficulty swings can generate clean-sheet projection deltas of $+1.0$ to $+1.5$ xP, the unconstrained 1-GW lineup evaluator viewed goalkeeper swaps as easy, high-efficiency gains, starving explosive outfield rotations.

### 2.3 Single-Gameweek Myopia & Reactive Zigzagging
`_evaluate_squad_lineup_xp` evaluated transfers against the immediate next gameweek only ($H=1$). In 2023-24, Haaland's 2-week knock in GW20 triggered a panic sale for Watkins, followed by Salah in GW21, followed by Richarlison in GW23. Evaluating only 1 week ahead creates reactive panic selling on temporary 1-to-2 week absences.

### 2.4 Suboptimal Bench Weighting During Chip Execution (Track B Deficit)
In Track B, V1.2 captured only **+24.4 pts** from chips across the season, whereas V1.0 captured **+59.6 pts**:
* During **Bench Boost**, all 4 bench players contribute 100% of their points, but V1.2 managed the squad assuming bench points were worth only $0.15\times$.
* During **Free Hit**, the entire squad lasts for only 1 gameweek; bench weight should be minimized ($0.05\times$) to concentrate maximum budget into the starting XI.

### Pillar 1: Candidate Pool Expansion (`max_results=25`)
Expanded `max_results` in `solve_transfers` calls from `5` to `25`. Empirical sweeps across $\{5, 15, 25, 50\}$ demonstrated that $25$ captures all high-leverage starting XI upgrades (+17.00 pts/season gain in Track A) while avoiding combinatorial slowdown and bench noise at higher values.

### Pillar 2: Goalkeeper Churn Suppression & Role-Specific Transfer Hurdles
1. **Position-Specific Net Gain Hurdles:**
   $$\text{Hurdle}(p) = \begin{cases} 
   1.50 \text{ pts}, & \text{if position is GOALKEEPER} \\
   0.50 \text{ pts}, & \text{if position is OUTFIELD (DEF/MID/FWD)}
   \end{cases}$$
2. **Goalkeeper Playing Security Invariant:**
   A goalkeeper transfer is rejected unless:
   * The incumbent goalkeeper has $P(\text{play}) < 0.50$ (injury, benching, departure); OR
   * The prospective replacement offers a projected 3-gameweek net gain $\ge 3.0$ points.
3. **Outcome:** GK transfers reduced from 5–10 to 2–3 per season across all historical seasons.

### Pillar 3: Multi-Gameweek Discounted Lineup Horizon ($H=3, \gamma=0.75$)
Rather than evaluating transfers on next week's points alone ($H=1$):
$$\Delta xP_{\text{rolling}} = \sum_{t=0}^{H-1} \gamma^t \cdot \left( \text{LineupXP}_{GW+t}(S_{\text{new}}) - \text{LineupXP}_{GW+t}(S_{\text{old}}) \right) - \text{HitCost}$$
where $H=3$ and $\gamma = 0.75$.
* Proactively captures multi-week fixture swings and green fixture runs.
* Suppresses panic sales of elite assets during short 1–2 week knocks.
* Strictly verified point-in-time: future projections sourced from deadline snapshot statistics and pre-season fixture schedules with zero future leakage.

### Pillar 4: Double Gameweek & Blank Gameweek Awareness
Natural emergence from Pillar 3: players playing 2 fixtures in a double gameweek accrue multi-fixture xP inside the rolling $H=3$ horizon, naturally surfacing DGW assets (e.g. Eze in GW34, Isak in GW37) without heuristics.

### Pillar 5: Dynamic Chip-Aware Bench Weighting
Dynamically adapt `bench_weight` to the operational context:
* **Standard Gameweeks:** `bench_weight = 0.15` (preserves starting XI budget concentration).
* **Free Hit Gameweeks:** `bench_weight = 0.05` (concentrates the entire £100m budget into the 11 on-pitch starters).
* **Pre-Bench Boost Window (1–2 GWs prior to BB deployment):** `bench_weight = 0.60` (rotates budget into playing bench enablers).
* **Bench Boost Gameweek:** `bench_weight = 0.99` (evaluates all 15 players equally, while avoiding the `bench_weight >= 1.0` legacy symmetric mode trap in `solve_strategic_squad`).
* **Zero Leakage:** Track A remains strictly identical (+0.0 pt change), proving zero chip contamination.

### Pillar 6: Resolution of Inert Long-Term Unavailability Mechanism (Option B)
The hand-curated registry was proven 100% inert in backtests (0 held assets affected across all 5 seasons). V1.2.5 cleanly removes purchase-pool exclusion for `DecisionEngineV125`, resulting in exact 0.00 drift across all 5 historical seasons while eliminating dead code surface area.

*(Note on §1.1(a) Captain Weight Formulation: In `_evaluate_squad_lineup_xp`, the additive captain term is $1.0 \times \text{cap\_p.expected\_points}$. Because the captain is already included in the starter sum with weight $1.0$, this totals exactly $2.0\times$ for the captain on matchday, matching official FPL rules.)*

### Formal Target Decision & Deferral Rationale

V1.2.5 achieved **2,046.60 net pts/season** in Track A (+20.80 pts vs V1.2) and **2,087.80 net pts/season** in Track B (+37.60 pts vs V1.2), while cutting cross-season variance to **±116.9** (a 43% variance reduction relative to V1.0's ±205.2). It closed **92% of the Track A performance deficit against V1.0**, trailing V1.0 by only **-1.8 pts**.

However, V1.2.5 falls short of the stretch target of $\ge 2,060.0$ Track A net points by -13.4 pts, and trails V1.0 by -20.2 pts in Track B.

**Root cause of the remaining gap:**
1. **Asymmetric Season Dynamics:** V1.2.5 strongly outperforms V1.0 in turbulent seasons (+91 pts in 2021-22, +87 pts in 2022-23) and matches V1.0 in 2025-26 (-1 pt). The remaining gap is entirely localized to the template-heavy outlier runs of 2023-24 and 2024-25, where V1.0's single-week greedy engine happened to hold high-variance outfielders through lucky single-gameweek spikes.
2. **Transfer Horizon vs Single-Asset Limits:** V1.2.5 evaluates transfers with a rolling 3-GW lineup horizon ($H=3, \gamma=0.75$), but is constrained to evaluate 1 transfer at a time ($K=1$). In seasons with rapid template price rises, multi-transfer moves ($K=2$, e.g. funding Haaland/Palmer by downgrading a defender) cannot be planned jointly across multiple gameweeks.
3. **Avoidance of Hindsight Overfitting:** Rather than artificially lowering transfer hurdles or tuning gamma specifically to fit 2023-24/2024-25 (which degrades 2021-22 and 2022-23), V1.2.5 locks in its principled point-in-time improvements.

**Decision:**
Achievement of the $\ge 2,060.0$ Track A target is formally **deferred to V1.3**. V1.3 introduces multi-transfer combinatorial planning ($K=2$) and LLM qualitative strategic risk assessment, providing the structural mechanism required to close the remaining gap in high-template seasons without overfitting.
