# V1.2 — Strategic Squad Balancing & Long-Term Unavailability

**Status:** Implementation complete — benchmark target not met; Pillar 2 verified inert (see §4.1)  
**Target Branch:** `v12`  
**Predecessor:** V1.1.5 (merged into `master`)  
**Next Milestone:** V1.2.5 (Lineup-Aware Transfer Refinements & Benchmark Target Achievement)  

**Primary Objective:** Close the empirical performance regression between V1.0 (2,048.4 pts) and V1.1/V1.1.5 (1,980.4 pts) by introducing asymmetric Starting XI vs Bench optimization in squad construction, lineup-aware transfer evaluation, and explicit point-in-time modeling of long-term player unavailability (multi-month bans and long-term injuries).

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation

In the audited 5-season historical benchmark (2021-22 through 2025-26), the system demonstrated:
- **V1.0 (Single-GW Decision Engine):** Achieved **2,048.4 net pts/season** in Track A (without chips).
- **V1.1 (Strategic Squad Optimization):** Achieved **1,983.0 net pts/season** in Track A (-65.4 pts vs V1.0).
- **V1.1.5 (Departure Engine & Hardening):** Achieved **1,980.4 net pts/season** in Track A (-68.0 pts vs V1.0), while recovering massive value in seasons with major departures (+99 pts in 2023-24).

### The Root Cause of the Regression
1. **Symmetric 15-Player Objective Pathology:**
   In V1.1 and V1.1.5, `solve_strategic_squad` uses a symmetric heuristic (1-opt and 2-opt) that treats all 15 roster spots with equal weight ($1.0\times$). In standard FPL, bench points score **zero** on matchday unless an auto-substitution occurs (~15% probability). Consequently, the solver routinely downgrades a premium starting attacker (e.g. £12.5m Salah/Haaland with 7.5 xP to an £8.0m asset with 5.5 xP, $\Delta = -2.0$) in order to upgrade the 3rd bench player from a £4.5m enabler (1.5 xP) to a £6.5m starter (3.8 xP, $\Delta = +2.3$).
   The unweighted solver registers a net gain of $+0.3$ xP, but the actual realized on-pitch return drops by $-2.0$ points per gameweek (plus lost captaincy bonus).
2. **Tolerance to Long-Term Unavailability:**
   Because V1.1/V1.1.5 builds a deep bench, the decision engine becomes excessively tolerant of injured or banned assets. When an asset suffers a multi-month suspension (e.g., Ivan Toney's 8-month ban in 2023-24 or Sandro Tonali's 10-month ban) or a long injury (e.g., ACL rupture), the engine relies on its bench cover rather than spending a transfer to liquidate the trapped £8.0m budget, causing months of dead capital stagnation. In contrast, V1.0's greedy 1-week horizon immediately purges anyone with 0 expected points next week.

### Pillar 1: Asymmetric Starting XI vs Bench Squad Balancing
- **Mathematical Formulation:**
  For any candidate 15-player squad $S$, the objective evaluated during 1-opt and 2-opt search must be:
  $$\text{Objective}(S) = \sum_{p \in \text{Starters}(S)} \text{Value}(p) + 0.8 \times \text{Value}(\text{Captain}(S)) + w_{\text{bench}} \times \sum_{p \in \text{Bench}(S)} \text{Value}(p)$$
  where $w_{\text{bench}} \in [0.10, 0.20]$ (calibrated default: $0.15$).
- **Search Loop Alignment:**
  Modify `solve_strategic_squad` in `src/fpl_manager/strategic_squad.py` so that candidate swaps are scored using the asymmetric lineup objective rather than the sum of raw player utilities.
- **Budget Concentration:**
  Forces the initial squad builder and wildcard solvers to allocate £82.0m–£84.0m into the 11 starters, keeping bench assets at budget-enabler levels (£4.0m–£4.5m) with high playing security.

### Pillar 2: Long-Term Unavailability Modeling
- **Unavailability Registry (`data/historical/unavailability_registry.json`):**
  Maintain verified point-in-time records of long-term absences:
  - Multi-month disciplinary/betting bans (e.g., Ivan Toney: May 17, 2023 – Jan 17, 2024; Sandro Tonali: Oct 27, 2023 – Aug 27, 2024).
  - Season-ending or prolonged multi-month injuries (>60 days expected absence).
  - Every registry entry requires a machine-checkable `known_from` timestamp, asserted against gameweek deadlines to ensure zero lookahead leakage into historical snapshots.
- **Explicit Signal Propagation (Post-Review Architecture):**
  Unavailability is computed as an explicit boolean verdict once at snapshot creation time (`build_historical_snapshot(..., apply_unavailability=True)` using the gameweek deadline as reference timestamp) and propagated immutably through domain objects:
  `HistoricalPlayerState.is_long_term_unavailable` $\to$ `ExpectedPointsProjection.is_long_term_unavailable` $\to$ `PlayerInfo.is_long_term_unavailable` $\to$ `PlayerOptInfo.is_long_term_unavailable`.
- **Model Invariant:**
  In `src/fpl_manager/models.py`, `is_long_term_unavailable(player, snapshot)`:
  - Criterion 0 short-circuits on the precomputed boolean attribute, guaranteeing point-in-time correctness without wall-clock dependency (`datetime.now()`) or relying on free-text `news` string prefix parsing as a transport mechanism.
  - Falls back to heuristic parsing (`evaluate_long_term_unavailable`) only for raw live-API objects with expected return date $> 35$ days away or severe injury/ban phrasing.
  - In `decide_transfers`, long-term unavailable squad assets trigger the same `dead_capital_penalty` (via `dead_capital_weight=3.0`) as Premier League departures, prioritizing their immediate liquidation.
  - In candidate selection (transfers, wildcards, initial squads), long-term unavailable players are strictly excluded from the purchase pool.

> **Measured effect: none.** These two rules are mutually pre-emptive — exclusion from the purchase pool means a long-term unavailable player never enters the squad, so the dead-capital penalty never has a subject to act on. Ablation confirms a contribution of exactly +0.0 pts/season across all 5 seasons (§4.1). The pillar is implemented and point-in-time correct, but does not currently influence any decision or any reported result.

### Pillar 3: Lineup-Aware Transfer Planning
- In `DecisionEngineV12`, transfer moves must be evaluated by their net impact on the **Starting XI expected points** rather than raw squad totals:
  $$\Delta xP_{\text{lineup}} = \text{LineupXP}(S_{\text{new}}) - \text{LineupXP}(S_{\text{old}}) - \text{Hits}$$
  This prevents burning free transfers or hits on sideways bench swaps that yield zero on-pitch points.

### Verdict
V1.2 improves on V1.1.5 by **+45.4 pts** in Track A (2,025.8 vs 1,980.4) and **+42.4 pts** in Track B (2,050.2 vs 2,007.8), and demonstrates the lowest variance of all versions (**±122.0**). However, V1.2 **does not meet the Pillar 4 exit criterion** of $\ge 2,050$ Track A mean net points (achieving 2,025.8, a shortfall of -24.2 pts), and still trails V1.0 by **22.6 pts in Track A** and **57.8 pts in Track B**.

V1.2 outperforms V1.0 in earlier, turbulent seasons (+106 pts in 2021-22 and +37 pts in 2022-23) through disciplined squad balancing and lineup-aware transfer evaluation. However, it underperforms V1.0 in high-scoring template seasons (2023-24, 2024-25, 2025-26) due to transfer funnel bottlenecks (pruning high-xP starting upgrades to top 5 unweighted candidates before lineup scoring) and goalkeeper transfer churn.

### 4.1 Pillar Ablation — Where the Gain Actually Comes From

The +45.4 pt Track A improvement over V1.1.5 was decomposed by ablation (5 seasons, GW 1–38, Track A):

| Pillar | Mechanism | Measured Contribution |
|---|---|---:|
| Pillar 1 | Asymmetric bench weighting (`bench_weight` 0.15 vs 1.0) | **+24.2 pts/season** |
| Pillar 2 | Long-term unavailability registry | **+0.0 pts/season** |
| Pillar 3 + dead capital | Lineup-aware transfer evaluation | **+21.2 pts/season** |

Per-season Pillar 1 deltas (`bench_weight=0.15` minus `bench_weight=1.0`): 2021-22 **+56**, 2022-23 **-21**, 2023-24 **+21**, 2024-25 **+64**, 2025-26 **+1**.

> **Pillar 2 is currently inert with respect to results.** Ablating the registry (running V1.2 with an empty `unavailability_registry.json`) reproduces V1.2's net points **exactly** in all five seasons — 1983 / 1867 / 2174 / 2120 / 1985, mean 2,025.8 — a delta of precisely 0.
>
> The signal itself is live and correctly propagated: `build_historical_snapshot(..., apply_unavailability=True)` flags 15–17 players per gameweek in 2023-24. The mechanism does not bite because the two consumers of the flag cannot interact. Long-term unavailable players are *excluded from the purchase pool*, so they never enter a V1.2 squad; and the `dead_capital_penalty` only applies to players *already in the squad*. The exclusion pre-empts the penalty, so no dead capital from long-term unavailability ever accumulates — corroborated by the Dead Capital Tx column reading 0 for V1.2 in every season above.
>
> Consequently, Pillar 2 should be read as **implemented and verified inert**, not as a contributor to V1.2's performance. The registry-selection-bias risk recorded in the handoff (§3, 6 of 9 entries in 2023-24) has no effect on any reported number for the same reason. Making the signal actually bite — or removing it — is deferred to V1.2.5.

**Formal Target Decision:**
Rather than tuning heuristic parameters ad-hoc or lowering the exit bar, V1.2 formally records this outcome and **defers achievement of the $\ge 2,050$ Track A net points target to V1.2.5**. The architectural specification for V1.2.5 (`docs/v1.2.5/v125.md`) is already scoped to address the candidate pool funnel, position transfer hurdles, and multi-GW lineup horizon, and additionally now owns resolving the inert Pillar 2 mechanism.

### Baseline Parity Verified
Predecessor versions (V0.9, V1.0, V1.1, V1.1.5) reproduce their canonical `master` branch benchmark ledgers exactly across all 5 historical seasons with zero drift:
- **V0.9:** Track A 1,983.6 (±212.8) / Track B 2,048.2 (±169.4)
- **V1.0:** Track A 2,048.4 (±205.2) / Track B 2,108.0 (±178.9)
- **V1.1:** Track A 1,983.0 (±122.4) / Track B 2,010.0 (±177.0)
- **V1.1.5:** Track A 1,980.4 (±145.2) / Track B 2,007.8 (±170.7)

**Parity Enforcement Architecture:**
During review, two high-severity baseline drift vectors were discovered and permanently resolved:
1. **Safe Default Weighting (fixing Issue 2):** `StrategicConstraints.bench_weight` now defaults to `1.0` (legacy symmetric mode matching `master`). Pre-V1.2 call sites across the codebase do not pass an explicit `bench_weight`, so they automatically run the canonical master algorithm. Only `DecisionEngineV12` and V1.2 backtest entry points explicitly opt in to `bench_weight=0.15`.
2. **Safe Snapshot Defaults (fixing Issue 6):** `build_historical_snapshot(..., apply_unavailability=False)` and `load_historical_strategic_players(..., apply_unavailability=False)` default to `False`. Hindsight registry data is strictly gated from contaminating pre-V1.2 evaluation and measurement paths.
3. **Deterministic Legacy Heuristics:** When `bench_weight >= 1.0`, `solve_strategic_squad` executes the exact pre-V1.2 symmetric per-player search algorithm, candidate pool caps, and objective evaluation, guaranteeing 100% byte-for-byte reproducibility against `master`.
