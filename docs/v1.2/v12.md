# V1.2 — Strategic Squad Balancing & Long-Term Unavailability

**Status:** Implementation complete — benchmark target not met (see §4)  
**Target Branch:** `v12`  
**Predecessor:** V1.1.5 (merged into `master`)  
**Next Milestone:** V1.3 (Multi-Provider LLM Expansion & Strategic Advisory)  

**Primary Objective:** Close the empirical performance regression between V1.0 (2,048.4 pts) and V1.1/V1.1.5 (1,980.4 pts) by introducing asymmetric Starting XI vs Bench optimization in squad construction, lineup-aware transfer evaluation, and explicit point-in-time modeling of long-term player unavailability (multi-month bans and long-term injuries).

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation

In the audited 5-season historical benchmark (2021-22 through 2025-26), the system demonstrated:
- **V1.0 (Single-GW Decision Engine):** Achieved **2,048.4 net pts/season** in Track A (without chips).
- **V1.1 (Strategic Squad Optimization):** Achieved **1,983.0 net pts/season** in Track A (-65.4 pts vs V1.0).
- **V1.1.5 (Departure Engine & Hardening):** Achieved **1,980.4 net pts/season** in Track A (-68.0 pts vs V1.0), while recovering massive value in seasons with major departures (+99 pts in 2023-24).

### The Root Cause of the Regression
1. **Symmetric 15-Player Objective Pathology:**
   In V1.1 and V1.1.5, `solve_strategic_squad` uses a symmetric heuristic (1-opt and 2-opt) that treats all 15 roster spots with equal weight ($1.0\times$). In standard FPL, bench points score **zero** on matchday unless an auto-substitution occurs (~15% probability). Consequently, the solver routinely downgrades a premium starting attacker (e.g. £12.5m Salah/Haaland with 7.5 xP to an £8.0m asset with 5.5 xP, $\Delta = -2.0$) in order to upgrade the 3rd bench player from a £4.5m enabler (1.5 xP) to a £6.5m starter (3.8 xP, $\Delta = +2.3$).
   The unweighted solver registers a net gain of $+0.3$ xP, but the actual realized on-pitch return drops by $-2.0$ points per gameweek (plus lost captaincy bonus).
2. **Tolerance to Long-Term Unavailability:**
   Because V1.1/V1.1.5 builds a deep bench, the decision engine becomes excessively tolerant of injured or banned assets. When an asset suffers a multi-month suspension (e.g., Ivan Toney's 8-month ban in 2023-24 or Sandro Tonali's 10-month ban) or a long injury (e.g., ACL rupture), the engine relies on its bench cover rather than spending a transfer to liquidate the trapped £8.0m budget, causing months of dead capital stagnation. In contrast, V1.0's greedy 1-week horizon immediately purges anyone with 0 expected points next week.

### Pillar 1: Asymmetric Starting XI vs Bench Squad Balancing
- **Mathematical Formulation:**
  For any candidate 15-player squad $S$, the objective evaluated during 1-opt and 2-opt search must be:
  $$\text{Objective}(S) = \sum_{p \in \text{Starters}(S)} \text{Value}(p) + 0.8 \times \text{Value}(\text{Captain}(S)) + w_{\text{bench}} \times \sum_{p \in \text{Bench}(S)} \text{Value}(p)$$
  where $w_{\text{bench}} \in [0.10, 0.20]$ (calibrated default: $0.15$).
- **Search Loop Alignment:**
  Modify `solve_strategic_squad` in `src/fpl_manager/strategic_squad.py` so that candidate swaps are scored using the asymmetric lineup objective rather than the sum of raw player utilities.
- **Budget Concentration:**
  Forces the initial squad builder and wildcard solvers to allocate £82.0m–£84.0m into the 11 starters, keeping bench assets at budget-enabler levels (£4.0m–£4.5m) with high playing security.

### Pillar 2: Long-Term Unavailability Modeling
- **Unavailability Registry (`data/historical/unavailability_registry.json`):**
  Maintain verified point-in-time records of long-term absences:
  - Multi-month disciplinary/betting bans (e.g., Ivan Toney: May 17, 2023 – Jan 17, 2024; Sandro Tonali: Oct 27, 2023 – Aug 27, 2024).
  - Season-ending or prolonged multi-month injuries (>60 days expected absence).
- **Model Invariant:**
  In `src/fpl_manager/models.py`, define `is_long_term_unavailable(player, snapshot)`:
  - Returns `True` if player is logged in the unavailability registry or has official news indicating an expected return date $> 35$ days away.
  - Long-term unavailable assets receive zero expected points across the entire 5-gameweek horizon.
  - In `decide_transfers`, long-term unavailable squad assets trigger the same `dead_capital_penalty` as Premier League departures, prioritizing their immediate liquidation.
  - In candidate selection (transfers, wildcards, initial squads), long-term unavailable players are strictly excluded from the purchase pool.

### Pillar 3: Lineup-Aware Transfer Planning
- In `DecisionEngineV12`, transfer moves must be evaluated by their net impact on the **Starting XI expected points** rather than raw squad totals:
  $$\Delta xP_{\text{lineup}} = \text{LineupXP}(S_{\text{new}}) - \text{LineupXP}(S_{\text{old}}) - \text{Hits}$$
  This prevents burning free transfers or hits on sideways bench swaps that yield zero on-pitch points.

### Pillar 4: Multi-Season Benchmark & Ledger Verification
- Evaluate V1.2 across all 5 historical seasons (`2021-22` through `2025-26`) in both Track A (without chips) and Track B (with chips).
- Validate whether V1.2 closes the -68.0 pt deficit against V1.0, targeting $\ge 2,050$ mean net points in Track A.

### Verdict
V1.2 improves on V1.1.5 by **+54.4 pts** (Track A) and **+55.4 pts** (Track B), and demonstrates the lowest variance of all versions (**±122.0**). However, V1.2 **does not meet the Pillar 4 target** of ≥2,050 Track A mean net points. It trails V1.0 by **22.6 pts in Track A** and **57.8 pts in Track B**. V1.2 outperforms V1.0 in the earlier seasons (2021-22, 2022-23) but underperforms in the most recent three seasons (2023-24, 2024-25, 2025-26). The variance reduction and improvements against V1.1.5 indicate successful stabilization of squad management, but the shortfall in 2023-24 and 2024-25 (where V1.0's single-GW greedy engine gained the most) points to remaining gaps in the lineup-aware transfer heuristics or the asymmetric weighting calibration.

### Baseline Drift Caveat
The V1.1 and V1.1.5 results in this benchmark run differ from the master branch ledger:
- **Master ledger:** V1.1 Track A 1,983.0 / Track B 2,010.0; V1.1.5 Track A 1,980.4 / Track B 2,007.8.
- **This run:** V1.1 Track A 1,992.0 / Track B 2,023.6; V1.1.5 Track A 1,971.4 / Track B 1,994.8.

This drift occurs because V1.2's changes to shared code—specifically `solve_strategic_squad` (asymmetric lineup scoring and 2-opt candidate cap reduction 30→25) and `optimizer.py` (dead-capital logic now including long-term unavailability) and `DecisionEngineV115` integration of `is_long_term_unavailable()`—also affect the behavior of older engines when executed in the same environment. V0.9 and V1.0 remain unchanged. Comparisons of V1.2 against V1.1/V1.1.5 should be read against this run, not the master ledger.
