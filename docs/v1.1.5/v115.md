## 1. Executive Summary

V1.1 delivered the strategic squad construction framework, dedicated Studio GUI, and reproducible multi-season evaluation.

V1.1.5 is a **hardening and operational-intelligence release**, not a new major product layer. Its purpose is to make the V1.1 strategic engine reliable when operating continuously across an entire FPL season and to establish a stronger experimental foundation before V1.2.

The release addresses four related areas:

1. **Premier League Departure Lifecycle**
   - Detect players who have permanently left the Premier League.
   - Prevent departed players from entering new candidate pools.
   - Prioritize the recovery of funds trapped in departed players.
   - Ensure departure handling is consistent across transfers, Wildcards, Free Hits, and strategic squad construction.

2. **Seasonal Chip State & Strategy**
   - Model the two independent FPL chip windows explicitly.
   - Track chip inventory across a complete season.
   - Evaluate chip deployment over multi-gameweek horizons rather than treating each gameweek independently.
   - Calibrate chip timing policies against historical seasons.
   - Introduce anti-pathology guardrails while keeping the policy configurable and measurable.

3. **Decision-State & Experiment Integrity**
   - Make every strategic/backtest decision reproducible from explicit state and configuration.
   - Record predictor, starting-state construction, decision-engine, objective, horizon, constraints, dataset, snapshot, seed, and experiment identity.
   - Make fallback behavior explicit rather than silently changing the evaluated engine.
   - Ensure reports can distinguish construction-time information from evaluation-time information.

4. **Multi-Version Historical Benchmark**
   - Quantify the observed behavior of V0.9, V1.0, V1.1, and V1.1.5 under a common historical evaluation framework.
   - Separate with-chip and without-chip evaluations.
   - Report per-season and aggregate results with complete provenance.
   - Use the benchmark to identify regressions, improvements, and interactions between architectural components.

## 2. Problem Statement & Product Thesis

### 2.1 The Premier League Departure Problem

In fantasy football, a player who has permanently left the Premier League is fundamentally different from an injured or rotating player:

- An injured player (`status = 'i'`) retains some possibility of returning.
- A rotation player (`status = 'a'`, low minutes) remains eligible to contribute.
- A departed player (`status = 'u'` or equivalent confirmed departure state) has no future Premier League scoring opportunity while consuming a squad slot and tying up funds.

A decision engine that represents all three cases merely through short-term xP can therefore make structurally incorrect transfer decisions.

#### Pathological Failure Mode

If a manager has:

- Player A: departed midfielder, £8.0m, 0.0 future xP.
- Player B: active defender, £5.0m, 1.8 future xP.

and the available transfer could instead improve another position by +2.7 xP, a purely local optimizer may defer selling Player A because the immediate points gain from replacing B is larger.

The strategic problem is not only expected points. It is that Player A represents **capital that cannot generate future points**.

### V1.1.5 Departure Invariants

1. A confirmed departed player must be represented as permanently ineligible for future Premier League scoring.
2. A departed player must receive explicit offload priority in transfer decisions when an affordable legal replacement exists.
3. Departed players must never enter new initial-squad, transfer, Wildcard, or Free Hit candidate pools.
4. Departure detection must use only information available in the relevant point-in-time snapshot.

---

### 2.2 Seasonal Chip Strategy

FPL chip decisions are inherently sequential: using a chip changes the set of options available later in the season.

A gameweek-local optimizer therefore cannot fully represent the opportunity cost of chip deployment.

The V1.1.5 engine explicitly models the two chip windows:

| Window | Gameweeks | Wildcard | Free Hit | Triple Captain | Bench Boost |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Window 1** | GW 1–19 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| **Window 2** | GW 20–38 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

Unused Window 1 chips do not roll into Window 2.

### Strategic Principle

The engine should evaluate chip deployment as part of a **seasonal state**, not as an isolated gameweek decision.

Examples of undesirable behavior that V1.1.5 should explicitly test:

1. Immediate Wildcard deployment shortly after construction of a strategic initial squad without sufficient structural deterioration.
2. Triple Captain deployment without an appropriately strong fixture opportunity.
3. Bench Boost deployment when the bench contains substantial playing uncertainty.
4. Free Hit deployment when the current squad already covers the relevant gameweek adequately.
5. Failure to preserve chip optionality for future high-value fixture structures.

These are **testable policy hypotheses**, not assumed universal truths. V1.1.5 should measure their historical behavior rather than treating fixed thresholds as already validated.

## 3. Detailed Technical Architecture

### 3.1 Pillar 1 — Premier League Departure & Dead Capital Engine

#### 3.1.1 Detection Specification

A player may be classified as `DEPARTED_LEAGUE` when the point-in-time snapshot provides sufficient evidence, including:

1. `status == "u"` where this represents official FPL unavailability caused by departure.
2. `chance_of_playing_this_round == 0` and `chance_of_playing_next_round == 0`, combined with explicit transfer/loan/contract-termination evidence.
3. Club identity no longer maps to an active Premier League team.
4. The player has no scheduled Premier League fixtures remaining while the season is ongoing, where this condition is sufficient to establish departure rather than another form of unavailability.

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)
class PlayerEligibilityStatus:
    player_id: int
    is_in_premier_league: bool
    status_code: str
    news: str
    dead_capital_penalty: float
```

Departure classification must remain point-in-time safe.

#### 3.1.2 Priority Sell Offloading

Departed players receive an explicit structural component in transfer evaluation:

$$
	ext{Objective}(	ext{transfer\_out})
=
\Delta	ext{xP}
+
	ext{DeadCapitalWeight}
\cdot
\mathbb{I}_{	ext{departed}}(p_{	ext{out}})
\cdot
rac{	ext{price\_tenths}(p_{	ext{out}})}{10.0}
$$

The weight must be calibrated or explicitly configured rather than treated as universally optimal.

The engine must also expose the structural reason for prioritizing the departure.

#### 3.1.3 Strict Buy-Side Filtering

In strategic squad construction, transfers, Wildcards, and Free Hits:

```python
eligible_pool = [
    p for p in candidate_pool
    if p.status != "u"
    and not is_departed_from_premier_league(p)
    and p.id not in departed_player_ids
]
```

No confirmed departed player may enter a newly constructed squad.

### 3.2 Pillar 2 — Seasonal Chip State & Calibration

#### 3.2.1 Two-Window Chip Inventory

```python
@dataclass
class SeasonalChipInventory:
    # Window 1 (GW 1–19)
    wildcard_w1: bool = True
    free_hit_w1: bool = True
    triple_captain_w1: bool = True
    bench_boost_w1: bool = True

    # Window 2 (GW 20–38)
    wildcard_w2: bool = True
    free_hit_w2: bool = True
    triple_captain_w2: bool = True
    bench_boost_w2: bool = True

    def available_chips(self, gameweek: int) -> set[str]:
        if 1 <= gameweek <= 19:
            chips = set()
            if self.wildcard_w1:
                chips.add("wildcard")
            if self.free_hit_w1:
                chips.add("free_hit")
            if self.triple_captain_w1:
                chips.add("triple_captain")
            if self.bench_boost_w1:
                chips.add("bench_boost")
            return chips

        if 20 <= gameweek <= 38:
            chips = set()
            if self.wildcard_w2:
                chips.add("wildcard")
            if self.free_hit_w2:
                chips.add("free_hit")
            if self.triple_captain_w2:
                chips.add("triple_captain")
            if self.bench_boost_w2:
                chips.add("bench_boost")
            return chips

        return set()
```

#### 3.2.2 Initial Policy Hypotheses

The following conditions are initial **policy hypotheses / guardrails** to be evaluated historically:

| Chip | Window | Initial Policy Signal | Guardrail to Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wildcard 1** | GW 1–19 | Squad degradation, fixture restructuring, or major availability shock | Avoid GW 2–4 deployment without substantial structural deterioration |
| **Triple Captain 1** | GW 1–19 | Strong premium-asset opportunity, particularly a high-value DGW | Avoid high rotation uncertainty |
| **Bench Boost 1** | GW 1–19 | Strong full-squad fixture opportunity | Avoid substantial zero-minute / low-minute bench exposure |
| **Free Hit 1** | GW 1–19 | Significant blank-gameweek coverage problem | Avoid deployment when existing squad already has sufficient coverage |
| **Wildcard 2** | GW 20–38 | Major fixture restructuring or substantial squad reconstruction | Preserve sufficient post-Wildcard planning horizon |
| **Free Hit 2** | GW 20–38 | Major blank-gameweek opportunity | Target genuine fixture deficit rather than a generic difficult week |
| **Triple Captain 2** | GW 20–38 | Strong cumulative fixture opportunity | Require sufficiently strong projected opportunity |
| **Bench Boost 2** | GW 20–38 | Strong full-squad fixture opportunity | Require sufficient projected bench contribution |

Thresholds may subsequently be calibrated from historical data, but must not be presented as empirically established until the relevant experiments have been completed.

#### 3.2.3 Seasonal Replay

Chip decisions must be evaluated through sequential simulation:

```text
GW N
  ↓
Current squad + bank + transfers + chip inventory
  ↓
Evaluate legal actions
  ↓
Select transfer / lineup / chip action
  ↓
Apply historical outcome
  ↓
Update squad + bank + chip inventory
  ↓
GW N+1
```

This preserves the opportunity cost created by earlier chip decisions.

### 3.3 Pillar 3 — Decision-State & Experiment Integrity

V1.1 demonstrated the importance of separating model correctness, decision-engine behavior, starting-state construction, and reporting.

V1.1.5 therefore requires explicit provenance for every strategic evaluation.

#### 3.3.1 Required Experiment Provenance

Each historical decision or experiment should record, where applicable:

```text
experiment_id
season
gameweek
deadline
starting_state_policy
starting_state_predictor_version
evaluation_predictor_version
decision_engine_version
objective
profile
horizon
constraints
solver_version
random_seed
dataset_version
snapshot_id
configuration_hash
```

#### 3.3.2 No Silent Fallback

If a requested V1.1/V1.1.5 engine cannot initialize:

- the failure must be recorded;
- the requested engine must remain identifiable;
- any fallback must be explicit;
- the report must distinguish requested engine from actual engine used.

A fallback must never silently turn a V1.1/V1.1.5 experiment into a V1.0 experiment.

#### 3.3.3 Point-in-Time Integrity

Starting-state construction and evaluation must distinguish:

- information available when the starting state was constructed;
- predictor version used to construct it;
- predictor version used for downstream evaluation.

Factorial experiments must not allow one factor to implicitly contain information from another factor.

### 3.4 Pillar 4 — Multi-Version Historical Benchmark Ledger

V1.1.5 introduces a common historical evaluation framework for:

```text
V0.9
Learned Participation Baseline
        ↓
V1.0
Canonical Decision Engine
        ↓
V1.1
Strategic Squad Engine
        ↓
V1.1.5
Departure + Seasonal Chip + Hardened Evaluation
```

The purpose is to **quantify observed differences between versions under controlled historical replay**, not to claim causal attribution beyond what the experimental design supports.

#### Dual-Track Reporting

For every historical season from 2021–22 through 2025–26:

1. **Track A — Without Chips:** Pure transfer and lineup evaluation to isolate the behavior of the underlying decision systems.
2. **Track B — With Chips:** Full-season sequential replay using the V1.1.5 seasonal chip state.

Reports should include:

- season total points;
- points per GW;
- transfer count;
- transfer hits;
- chip usage;
- captain performance;
- bench regret;
- zero-minute starts;
- departure-related decisions;
- final squad state;
- bank evolution;
- per-GW decision ledger.

No single aggregate metric should be treated as sufficient evidence of superiority.

## 5. Testing & Research Quality

V1.1.5 must retain the V1.1 standard of deterministic, reproducible testing.

### 5.1 Unit & Integration Testing

Required coverage includes:

- departure classification;
- departure precedence;
- candidate-pool exclusion;
- dead-capital transfer prioritization;
- chip inventory accounting;
- Window 1 expiry;
- chip legality;
- seasonal sequential replay;
- fallback transparency;
- provenance completeness;
- deterministic experiment identity;
- point-in-time information boundaries.

### 5.2 Synthetic Ground Truth

Where a strategy or invariant can be tested against a known optimum, use bounded synthetic cases with an independent reference implementation.

This should include:

- chip-state transitions;
- departure candidate filtering;
- transfer decisions involving dead capital;
- horizon calculations;
- seasonal chip opportunity cost where computationally feasible.

### 5.3 Historical Evaluation

Historical experiments must report:

- exact seasons and gameweeks;
- predictor version;
- starting-state version;
- decision-engine version;
- objective/profile;
- horizon;
- constraints;
- dataset/snapshot;
- seed;
- configuration hash;
- experiment ID.

### 5.4 Report Integrity

Generated reports must be reproducible from the corresponding code and configuration.

Analytical conclusions must distinguish:

- observed results;
- model-derived quantities;
- heuristic interpretations;
- calibrated policies;
- causal claims, where supported.

Avoid claims such as "proves", "optimal", or "caused by" unless the experiment design actually establishes those properties.

## 6. Acceptance Criteria & Release Gate

### P0 — Correctness

1. **Departure Invariant:** No confirmed departed player can enter a newly constructed squad or candidate pool.
2. **Departure Offload:** A departed player is explicitly recognized as dead capital and considered for priority offloading when a legal replacement exists.
3. **Point-in-Time Safety:** Departure classification uses only information available at the relevant snapshot.
4. **Chip State:** Chip inventories are correctly separated between GW 1–19 and GW 20–38.
5. **Chip Expiry:** Unused Window 1 chips do not become available in Window 2.
6. **Legal Actions:** The seasonal engine never executes an illegal chip action.

### P0 — Reproducibility

7. **No Silent Fallback:** Requested and actual engine versions are always distinguishable.
8. **Complete Provenance:** Historical strategic decisions contain sufficient metadata for deterministic reconstruction.
9. **Deterministic Replay:** Re-running an identical experiment produces the same decision ledger and aggregate results.

### P1 — Historical Evaluation

10. **Multi-Season Benchmark:** Five historical seasons are evaluated under a common framework.
11. **Version Ledger:** V0.9, V1.0, V1.1, and V1.1.5 configurations are explicitly identified.
12. **Dual Track:** With-chip and without-chip evaluations are separately reported.
13. **Report Reproducibility:** Markdown and JSON reports can be regenerated from code/configuration.
14. **No Unsupported Claims:** Conclusions accurately distinguish measured results from interpretation.

### P1 — Product Integration

15. **CLI Integration:** Strategic backtests support seasonal chip state and version comparison.
16. **GUI Integration:** Studio surfaces departure status, chip state, and relevant decision provenance.
17. **Documentation:** Roadmap and version documentation reflect the final implementation.

### Release Gate

18. **Zero Test Regressions:** Full test suite passes.
19. **Historical Replay Completeness:** All required benchmark runs complete successfully.
20. **Repository Consistency:** Code, tests, reports, documentation, and roadmap describe the same V1.1.5 behavior.
21. **V1.2 Boundary:** V1.1.5 does not introduce the broader multi-provider/extended-LLM scope reserved for V1.2.
