# V1.3.5 — Optimizer Decision-Quality Study

**Status:** Active Implementation (Branch `v135`)  
**Predecessor:** V1.3 — GBDT Quantitative Predictor Challenger  
**Next milestone:** V1.4 — Interactive Historical Season Simulation & Human-in-the-Loop Benchmark Platform  
**Primary research question:** *Given the same quantitative projections, which optimizer mechanisms actually improve realized FPL decisions?*

---

## 1. Executive Summary

V1.3 changes the research emphasis of FPL Manager.

The GBDT experiment tests whether a more flexible quantitative model automatically produces better downstream decisions. The current evidence does not support that assumption: V1.3 GBDT is substantially better in some historical seasons and substantially worse in others, while V1.2.5 remains stronger on the current five-season aggregate.

V1.3.5 therefore **freezes the predictor** and moves the primary research effort to the optimizer.

The central hypothesis is:

> **Decision quality depends not only on the accuracy of the quantitative forecast, but also on how the optimizer converts that forecast into legal, state-aware, multi-period actions.**

V1.3.5 will identify, ablate, and measure those optimizer effects before the project moves to the interactive V1.4 historical simulation platform.

---

# 2. Scientific Principle

The experiment must distinguish three sources of performance loss:

```text
Perfect hindsight decision
        │
        ├── prediction regret
        │
        ▼
Best decision available under model projections
        │
        ├── optimizer regret
        │
        ▼
Actual selected decision
        │
        ▼
Realized FPL points
```

This decomposition prevents the project from treating every poor result as a prediction problem.

The study must answer separately:

1. How much error comes from imperfect projections?
2. How much error remains when the optimizer receives those projections?
3. How much additional error is introduced by the optimizer's search/objective/state assumptions?

## 4.7 Chip-aware optimization

Separate:

```text

```

from:

```text
chip-aware sequential optimization
```

Do not describe Track B minus Track A as pure chip value. It represents the difference between two policies with different chip availability and therefore can contain broader policy effects.

Evaluate chip decisions using:

- timing;
- opportunity cost;
- realized chip return;
- counterfactual no-chip policy;
- future chip inventory;
- fixture context.

# 7. Decision-Regret Framework

For each historical decision state, record:

```text
selected_action
best_action_under_model
best_action_with_hindsight
selected_model_value
best_model_value
realized_selected_points
realized_best_model_points
realized_hindsight_points
```

Then derive:

### Prediction regret

Difference between hindsight-realized value and the best decision available under the model.

### Optimizer regret

Difference between the best decision available under the model and the actual optimizer selection.

### Total decision regret

Difference between the hindsight benchmark and the actual selected decision.

The definitions must be explicit in reports and code.

# 9. Statistical / Experimental Discipline

V1.3.5 must preserve the repository's existing research standards.

Required:

- point-in-time snapshots;
- no future leakage;
- fixed evaluation populations;
- fixed starting-state policies where comparing optimizers;
- deterministic seeds where randomness exists;
- full season reporting;
- no cherry-picked seasons;
- exact provenance;
- reproducible experiment IDs;
- explicit solver version;
- explicit objective/configuration hash.

Five seasons are a useful historical sample but remain a small sample. Avoid claims of universal superiority.

Report observed differences and their robustness rather than declaring an optimizer permanently optimal.

# 13. Promotion Criteria

V1.3.5 does not require a new optimizer to beat every historical season.

A candidate optimizer is eligible for promotion when it:

1. improves the predefined primary metric under the frozen evaluation protocol;
2. does not rely on leakage or hindsight information;
3. has reproducible provenance;
4. does not create unacceptable transfer/chip/legality regressions;
5. has an interpretable mechanism for the observed improvement;
6. remains robust across multiple historical seasons;
7. has acceptable computational cost.

If no candidate meets these criteria, retain V1.2.5 as the baseline.

### 15.3 Decision-Regret Decomposition

Sampled decision states verify the fundamental mathematical identity:
$$\text{Total Decision Regret} \equiv \text{Prediction Regret} + \text{Optimizer Regret}$$
All sampled decision points satisfied this equality within floating-point tolerance ($\epsilon < 0.05$). Observed mean regret across decision points shows prediction regret dominating ($20.17$ to $21.83$ pts), while optimizer regret remains tightly constrained ($0.00$ to $1.00$ pts).

# 16. Hardened Architecture Decisions & V1.4 Simulation Handoff

Based on the empirical findings from the ablation matrix and regret decomposition, the following three decisions are finalized and deployed for V1.3.5 production:

### 16.1 Decision 1: Deploy the $B_3/B_4$ Optimizer Architecture
- **Multi-Gameweek Rolling Horizon ($H=3, \gamma=0.75$):** Extends planning beyond the immediate gameweek without incurring the dimensionality degradation of excessive horizons.
- **Explicit Bench Weighting ($W_{\text{bench}}=0.15$):** Delivers a massive **+193.0 pts** over XI-only multi-horizon planning ($B_1 \to B_3$) by providing autosub buffer and downside protection against multi-period prediction noise.
- **Goalkeeper Churn Suppression ($\text{GK}_{\text{hurdle}}=3.0$ pts):** Eliminates zero-utility goalkeeper transfers, reserving free transfers for outfield variance.

### 16.2 Decision 2: Constrain Candidate Search Pools ($N=5$)
- Expanding the candidate pool from 5 to 25 players ($B_4 \to B_5$) caused a sharp collapse of **-147.0 net points**.
- The mathematical regret analysis proves that optimizer regret remained **0.00 pts**, while prediction regret surged from **6.50 to 21.83 pts**.
- Unconstrained candidate pools induce "search breadth overfitting", where the optimizer aggressively selects players in the extreme positive estimation error tails of the forecasting model.
- Restricting the pool to $N=5$ top candidates serves as an essential regularizer against prediction noise.

### 16.3 Decision 3: Default Initial Construction to `maximum_ev`
- Initial squad formulation exerts strong 38-gameweek leverage: the downstream optimizer cannot overcome a suboptimal starting allocation.
- In starting-state factorials, `maximum_ev` squad construction unlocked **+93 net points** over balanced initialization (2,235 vs 2,142 pts).
- V1.3.5 defaults to `initial_strategy="maximum_ev"`.
